# On-Chain Research

> Blockchain gives you something traditional businesses don't: public evidence.

Use on-chain data to test project claims against observable activity.

## What to Inspect

- Transactions
- Token holders
- Contract interactions
- Treasury wallets
- Token transfers
- Liquidity pools
- Contract ownership
- Whale activity
- Deployer history
- Protocol usage where relevant

## Claim Verification

If a project claims **100,000 users**, ask:

> Where is the evidence?

Do not treat a claimed number as verified simply because it appears on the website or social media.

## Liquidity Investigation

Ask:

> If everybody wants to sell tomorrow, where does the liquidity come from?

Check:

- Liquidity size
- Where liquidity is held
- Who controls LP tokens
- Whether liquidity is locked and for how long
- Whether liquidity can be removed
- Whether liquidity is concentrated
- Whether liquidity is sufficient for the claimed market size

> **Liquidity locked ≠ project safe.**

## Deployer and Ownership History

Where applicable, inspect:

- Deployer history
- Contract ownership changes
- Related wallets
- Large transfers
- Treasury movements
- Contract interactions

## Evidence Log

For each material claim, record:

| Claim | Evidence source | Finding | Confidence/notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example: user count | Explorer / official source | Pending verification | Need methodology |
| Example: contract ownership | Block explorer | Verified owner address | Record timestamp |
| Example: liquidity | DEX analytics | Pending verification | Check LP control |
